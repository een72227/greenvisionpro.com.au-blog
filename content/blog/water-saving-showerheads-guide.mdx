## Summary

Water-saving showerheads reduce the volume of water used per shower without necessarily
reducing comfort, using technology such as aeration or pressure-compensating flow restrictors.
Because heating water requires energy, using less hot water can also reduce energy consumption
— making water-saving showerheads a fitting that touches both the water bill and the energy
bill.

## How water-efficient showerheads work

Most efficient showerheads use one of a few approaches:

- **Aeration**, which mixes air into the water stream to maintain a fuller-feeling spray while
  using less water
- **Turbulence technology**, which manipulates water flow patterns to feel more forceful at a
  lower flow rate
- **Flow restrictors**, which physically limit the maximum flow rate through the fitting

The right choice often depends on your home's water pressure and personal preference for spray
type.

## Understanding WELS ratings

The Water Efficiency Labelling and Standards (WELS) scheme rates showerheads and other plumbing
fixtures from one to six stars. A higher rating generally means lower water flow for a given
level of performance. When comparing showerheads, checking the WELS rating and litres-per-minute
(L/min) flow rate gives a clearer picture than marketing claims alone.

## Why this matters for hot water systems

Water heating is one of the larger energy uses in many Australian homes. Reducing hot water
demand — through fittings like efficient showerheads — works alongside, rather than instead of,
upgrading to a more efficient hot water system such as a heat pump. Addressing both the fitting
and the system tends to have a more meaningful combined effect than either change alone.

## What to look for when choosing a showerhead

- A **WELS rating of 3 stars or higher** for a reasonable balance of comfort and efficiency
- Compatibility with your **existing plumbing fittings**, to avoid additional installation work
- **Cleanable nozzles**, since mineral build-up can affect spray pattern over time
- Whether the model suits your household's **water pressure**, particularly in homes with mains
  pressure variability

## Installation considerations

Water-saving showerheads are typically straightforward to install and, in many cases, can
replace an existing showerhead without specialist plumbing work. However, for properties with
older plumbing or non-standard fittings, a professional installation can help ensure a proper
seal and consistent performance.
