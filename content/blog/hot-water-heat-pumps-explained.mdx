## Summary

A heat pump hot water system heats water by extracting heat from the surrounding air, rather
than generating heat directly through an electric element or gas burner. This process typically
requires less electricity to produce the same amount of hot water compared with a conventional
electric storage system, which is why heat pump systems are widely promoted as an efficient
option for Victorian households.

## How a heat pump hot water system works

The system works on the same principle as a reverse cycle air conditioner or a refrigerator, but
in reverse: a refrigerant absorbs heat from the outside air, and a compressor increases the
temperature of that heat before transferring it into the water stored in the tank. Because the
electricity is mainly used to move heat rather than create it, the system can deliver more heat
energy into the water than the electrical energy it consumes.

## Comparing hot water system types

- **Electric resistance storage** — heats water directly with an element; simple but typically
  higher ongoing energy use
- **Gas storage or instantaneous** — uses gas to heat water, with running costs tied to gas
  prices
- **Solar hot water** — uses solar collectors, often with an electric or gas booster
- **Heat pump** — extracts ambient heat from the air to heat water, generally with lower
  electricity use than resistance heating for an equivalent volume of hot water

The right choice depends on your household's hot water usage, available space, existing
connections (electricity vs gas) and budget.

## What to consider before switching

- **Space and placement** — heat pump units need adequate airflow and are typically installed
  outdoors or in a well-ventilated area
- **Household hot water demand** — larger households may need a larger tank capacity or a system
  sized accordingly
- **Existing connection type** — switching from gas to an electric heat pump may involve
  additional considerations depending on your property
- **Noise** — as with any compressor-based appliance, placement relative to living and sleeping
  areas is worth discussing with your installer

## Installation and servicing

Installation should be carried out by a licensed and, where relevant, accredited installer
familiar with the specific product and local regulations. Ongoing servicing — including checking
the anode, valves and refrigerant components — helps maintain performance and extend the
system's operating life.
