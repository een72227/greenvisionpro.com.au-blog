## Summary

Reducing household energy use generally comes down to two things working together: everyday
habits that cost nothing to adopt, and longer-term upgrades to equipment and the building itself.
Below are practical, realistic steps Victorian households can consider — none of which are
guaranteed to produce a specific saving, since usage patterns, home size and climate all play a
role.

## Heating and cooling habits

1. **Set a moderate thermostat range.** Every extra degree of heating or cooling typically
   increases energy use. A commonly suggested range is around 18–20°C for heating and 24–26°C
   for cooling, adjusted to comfort.
2. **Only condition rooms in use**, using zoning where your system supports it, rather than
   heating or cooling the whole home unnecessarily.
3. **Close doors and curtains** in unused rooms and overnight to help retain conditioned air.
4. **Service your system regularly** so it isn't working harder than necessary due to blocked
   filters or low refrigerant.

## Hot water habits

5. **Take shorter showers** where practical, since water heating is a meaningful energy use in
   many homes.
6. **Fix dripping hot water taps** promptly — a small, continuous drip adds up over time.
7. **Wash clothes in cold water** where appropriate for the fabric and detergent used.

## Everyday appliance habits

8. **Switch appliances off at the wall** rather than leaving them on standby, particularly
   entertainment systems and chargers.
9. **Run full loads** in dishwashers and washing machines rather than partial loads.
10. **Use natural light and ventilation** where possible before switching to lighting or
    cooling.

## Habits vs upgrades: where to start

Habits are free to start today but have a ceiling — there's only so much a thermostat setting
can achieve if the underlying heating system or building envelope is inefficient. Upgrades
address the equipment and structure itself, which is where programs like Victorian Energy
Upgrades can help make certain improvements more accessible for eligible households.

11. **Check your home for draughts** around doors, windows and vents — see our

12. **Review your hot water system's age and type** — older electric storage systems are
    generally less efficient than modern heat pump alternatives; see our
    [hot water heat pump guide](/blog/hot-water-heat-pumps-explained).

## Building a longer-term plan

Rather than tackling everything at once, many households find it useful to prioritise the
change most relevant to their situation — for example, addressing an ageing hot water system
before a heating system that's still performing well. Our team can help assess your home and
suggest a sensible order of priorities based on your specific circumstances.
