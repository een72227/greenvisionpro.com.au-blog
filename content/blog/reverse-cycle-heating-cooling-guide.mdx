## Summary

Reverse cycle air conditioning uses a heat pump to both heat and cool a home, rather than
generating heat directly. Because it moves existing heat rather than creating it from scratch,
a well-sized reverse cycle system can be significantly more efficient than traditional electric
resistance heaters or older gas systems — though actual running costs depend on your home,
climate and usage habits.

## How reverse cycle systems work

A reverse cycle unit contains a refrigerant that absorbs heat from one location and releases it
in another. In heating mode, it extracts latent heat from outside air — even on cool days — and
transfers it indoors. In cooling mode, the process runs in reverse, extracting heat from inside
air and releasing it outside.

This is fundamentally different to a system that generates heat by burning gas or running
current through a resistive element, which is why heat pump-based systems can deliver more
heating (or cooling) energy than the electrical energy they consume.

## Why efficiency ratings matter

Reverse cycle systems in Australia are rated using a star system, covering both heating and
cooling performance. A higher star rating generally indicates the unit delivers more heating or
cooling output per unit of electricity consumed. When comparing systems, it's worth looking at:

- The **heating and cooling star ratings** separately, since they can differ
- The **capacity (kW)** relative to the size of the room or zone being conditioned
- Whether the unit is a **single split**, **multi-split**, or **ducted** system

## Choosing the right size for your home

An undersized system will struggle to maintain temperature on extreme days and may run
constantly, while an oversized system can cycle on and off frequently, reducing comfort and
efficiency. Sizing should account for room dimensions, ceiling height, insulation levels, window
orientation and local climate zone — not just floor area alone.

## Ducted vs split systems

Split systems condition individual rooms or zones and are generally lower cost to install.
Ducted systems distribute conditioned air through ceiling or floor ducts to multiple rooms from
a single outdoor unit, offering more even whole-home comfort but at a higher upfront cost.
Zoning controls can help a ducted system avoid conditioning unused rooms.

## Maintenance that protects efficiency

- Clean or replace filters regularly, as blocked filters reduce airflow and efficiency
- Keep outdoor units clear of leaves, dust and vegetation
- Schedule a professional service periodically to check refrigerant levels and electrical
  components
- Address unusual noises or reduced performance promptly rather than waiting for a full failure
